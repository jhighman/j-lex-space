# Lo Se Munje · eight takes

Eight recorded takes of the same song, *Lo Se Munje*, each drawn by a
different lens, made between 12 and 16 October 2026 in Studio B and
brought into the workbench on 20 October as examples for the Phase
Transition review (issue #9). Each is 1:25: five parts, four key changes.
A frame from forty seconds in sits beside each take.

| take | title | lens on air at 0:40 | what the frame shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lo-se-munje-1.mp4` | I. What it heard | Atlas | the song's timeline as a ring of parts, Bridson's growth filling the body inside it |
| `lo-se-munje-2.mp4` | II. The key is the shape | Aizawa | a shape that never leaves and never repeats, traced by motes, with the spectrum as a ring of spikes |
| `lo-se-munje-3.mp4` | III. The same reading, as a body | Loft | a body written as spines and fans; the motes gather into a figure that stands |
| `lo-se-munje-4.mp4` | IV. Two kinds of time | Guard | particles cross a threshold and are counted; the body decides what crosses |
| `lo-se-munje-5.mp4` | V. The rules are the picture | Schema | a wireframe kept by generative rules, with its signal path, emotion emitter and philosophy emitter drawn as panels |
| `lo-se-munje-6.mp4` | VI. The instrument reading itself | Schema | the same, with an ignorance emitter: words not read, language not read, the camera never opened |
| `lo-se-munje-7.mp4` | VII. Where two things coincide | Hopf | two families of opposite-handed rings, each lit where the music is |
| `lo-se-munje-8.mp4` | VIII. An alphabet with a memory | Sigil | an alphabet of fifteen glyphs, one per feature of the signal, laid out as a line |

Each take carries its captions: the API calls that drove it, top left,
and the lens's own label and note, top right, so a viewer can see what was
asked of the body and when.

## Where they come from

**Studio B**, a subsection of Milo & Me (`ARAPAHOE/studio-b` in that
project's repository, which is private). Studio B is a two-dimensional
playground for songs and visual ideas: a song is dropped in and read once
end to end, an idea is tried as a lens in a few hundred lines of canvas,
played on two decks, and recorded. The `visualizer-demo/` in this
workbench is the public cut of the same instrument with fourteen lenses;
Studio B carries those and more. Six of the eight takes here use lenses
that exist only there: Aizawa, Loft, Guard, Schema, Hopf, Sigil. The two
Atlas takes could be re-run from this repository; the other six can be
re-run from Studio B, where each take has its orchestration
(`orchestrations/lo-se-munje-i.json` through `-viii.json`) and a note on
the eight readings (`orchestrations/lo-se-munje.md`).

Milo & Me is kept out of this workbench on purpose. In Jeff's words, that
project is undisciplined ludic elaboration and this workbench is for
serious research; the takes cross over as examples of what the instrument
has drawn, and the instrument stays where it is.

**Why the song is not in English.** A logic guard, chosen before the
takes were made: a song whose words the listener understands lets the
language influence the interpretive quality of the research, because the
reader of the image would be reading the words too. A song in a language
neither the instrument nor the reader can parse keeps the reading to the
sound. The instrument's side of this is recorded in Studio B's note: the
signal it draws from carries twelve pitch classes, onsets, loudness and
brightness and no words, so a song in a language it cannot read is read
exactly as completely as one it could. That is why Pulley, the one lens
that reads a poem, is absent from all eight.

The guard also opens a line of inquiry the prompt makes room for: the
dialect and sound properties of languages themselves as musical
features, chanting in eastern dialects among them, read by the same
instrument that cannot tell what any of them mean.

## Provenance, stated plainly

- **The files.** Transcoded from the originals (1920×1080 at 60 fps,
  about 15 Mb/s, 85–171 MB each) to 1080p at 30 fps, H.264 CRF 23, AAC
  160 kb/s, so each sits under GitHub's file limits. The originals stay
  on Jeff's machine. Nothing else was changed.
- **The song.** *Lo Se Munje* is Jeff's. How it was made is part of the
  disclosure the programme asks for and is to be stated with the
  submission, not assumed here.
- **The caveat.** The same process built the instrument, recorded the
  takes and wrote this note.
