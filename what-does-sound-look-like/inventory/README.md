# The inventory, sorted: does the sound drive the space, or do symbols move to the sound?

Context for the Phase Transition review (issue #9), added 2026-10-20 after
the Step 1 post. That post listed everything tried and named five
candidates. This folder shows the rest, one frame each, and says why each
was sorted where it was.

The premise of the visualizer, stated plainly: it was built as expression
more than as sound driving space. Some of what looks best in it has the
least science in it. It is symbols that move to the sound, and they are
beautiful, and that is a different thing from the sound organising the
image. The sort below separates the two, using the translation test as
Lex wrote it in the issue: with the caption gone, does a trace of the
research remain?

Three bins:

- **The sound drives the space.** Remove the caption and the figure still
  carries something of the sound: an interval, a spectrum, a frequency.
- **Symbols move to the sound.** The figure is chosen beforehand and the
  music strikes it. Rhythm sets its clock; a key change is an event. A
  metronome would draw the same picture.
- **Not sound at all.** Shapes from a modelled state, listed only so a
  morphology is not reinvented.

How the frames were taken: *Editions*, playing from 0:30, screenshot at
0:42, with captions on (the API calls top left, the lens's own note top
right) and the deck chrome hidden, so each looks as a take would. The
lab frames are a fresh sample of 144 sessions of 120 s. The Unframeable
Now frames are from Kate's day-2 playtest, not re-rendered.

## 1. The sound drives the space (the candidates)

### Lissajous
![](lens-lissajous.png)
The two notes of the sounding interval are the two oscillators; the
figure is their ratio (6:5 in the frame, a minor third). The interval
*is* the shape. The one lens where the relation is the same one Lissajous
drew with tuning forks and a mirror.

### Stern–Brocot
![](lens-sternbrocot.png)
The tree of every fraction, and the sounding interval found in it a beat
at a time: consonant near the root, dissonant deep. The frame reads
6/5, five turns from 1/1, four walked. A path in a tree of ratios is a
trace of an interval, caption or not, though it needs a reader who knows
what a fraction is.

### Prism
![](lens-prism.png)
The chord split into twelve wedges, each as wide as its note is loud,
turned one wedge per beat and ordered by fifths or chromatically on a
key change. The spectrum, ordered. Its trace is the proportion of the
wedges; the turning is the only part that is only rhythm.

### The plate
See `../index.html`, the folder this sits in: a frequency, a plate, a
figure where the matter stops moving. The literal relation, and the
reason it is the first candidate. Its limits are stated on its own page.

## 2. Symbols move to the sound (left out of the candidate table)

### The body alone (no lens)
![](lens-none.png)
The instrument's idle: a spiral of motes inside a shell with twelve
spokes, lit as the notes sound. The stage every lens sits on. Caption
gone, a turning disc.

### Tide
![](lens-tide.png)
Beats pour water in, time drains it, a key change brings a spring tide.
The trace is a level rising and falling: energy, not frequency.

### Frost
![](lens-frost.png)
Beats grow a six-armed crystal; silence melts it; a key change half-thaws
it. The six-fold symmetry is the lens's choice, not the sound's.

### Kiln
![](lens-kiln.png)
Beats heat the body and quiet cools it; its colour is its temperature.
The third accumulator. If one of the three is ever wanted it is as a
second deck under an interval lens, so the energy reading sits beneath
the frequency reading, which is what the paired-take candidate is for.

### Garden
![](lens-garden.png)
Beats grow a stem; each key change plants a new one in its own colour
and the old ones stay. The key change is visible as an event with a
colour, not as a direction. Nothing says which key, what interval, or
how far.

### Swarm
![](lens-swarm.png)
Each beat scatters the flock and it regathers; a key change moves where
it gathers. A flocking rule; the music only strikes it.

### Bridson
![](lens-bridson.png)
Poisson-disc growth a step each beat; a key change clears a patch and
grows it back in its own colour. The geometry comes from the sampling
algorithm; the music is its clock and the key change its eraser. It
survives inside Atlas as the fill of the timeline, where it belongs.

### Ripple
![](lens-ripple.png)
The nearest miss, and the one to argue about. A stone drops where the
loudest note is on the circle of twelve, so position carries pitch
class, and the rings cross where notes follow each other. But every
stone makes the same ring: the trace is *where*, not *what*. Second rank
behind Prism and Lissajous rather than wrong. If the twelve-note circle
read literally is wanted, this is the lens to bring back.

### Globe
![](lens-globe.png)
The twelve notes as longitude bands on a turning globe, lit by a sun
that sits where the key is. A conceit chosen beforehand with the notes
painted on. Caption gone, a planet with lights.

### Pulley
![](lens-pulley.png)
Herbert's "The Pulley" as forces round a centre, Lex's design: each verb
pushes, pulls, holds or tosses the body home, and the music applies the
current verb. Strong for the poem, not for frequency. Two more reasons
it is out of this review: the prompt is sound to image, not text to
image; and a lens that reads words is what the language guard removes.
It is why Pulley is absent from all eight *Lo Se Munje* takes. Reserved,
not rejected.

### Sunburst
![](lens-sunburst.png)
The song as rings of parts and bars, zooming where the music is. A chart
of the analyser's segmentation: structure in time, not vibration.
Honest and useful as a map.

### Atlas
![](lens-atlas.png)
The same map with Bridson's growth filling it, in the key heard. Take 1
of the eight, and still in play inside the paired-take candidate as the
structural layer behind an interval lens. On its own it reads as a
diagram.

### The six Studio B lenses, sorted from their takes
Aizawa, Loft, Guard, Schema, Hopf and Sigil are in
`visualizer-demo/examples/lo-se-munje/`, each with a frame, and the
instrument that draws them is not in this workbench. From the takes:
Aizawa, Loft, Guard and Schema are symbols moving to the sound (an
attractor, a body, a threshold, a wireframe, each struck by the music);
Hopf is the same with a spectrum lit onto rings; Sigil is a notation,
fifteen glyphs for fifteen features of the signal, which is closer to the
first bin than to the second and is the one of the six worth re-reading
under the test.

## 3. Not sound at all

### The lab's renderings
Each draws the modelled state of a session under The Unframeable Now's
premise: attention, calm, agency, the minutes. No sound in any of them.

| view | frame | what it draws |
|---|---|---|
| Discs | ![](lab-discs.png) | one eroding disc per persona × pace at the median end state |
| Bands | ![](lab-bands.png) | each dimension over time, median with its p10–p90 band |
| Strips | ![](lab-strips.png) | every session's end state as a dot, one strip per dimension |
| Morph | ![](lab-morph.png) | a superformula blob, round when whole, spiked as the dimensions fall |
| Brain | ![](lab-brain.png) | the four dimensions on a top-down brain, by concept not anatomy |
| Petals | ![](lab-petals.png) | every session as a four-petal glyph on a grid |
| 3D paths | ![](lab-path3d.png) | each session as a path through attention × calm × agency |
| 3D surface | ![](lab-surface3d.png) | wholeness as a height field; the bad day is a trench |
| Lineage | ![](lab-lineage.png) | one session as a tangled tree of causes |

Worth remembering: Petals and Morph are parametric shapes from four
numbers, and the plate's figure is a parametric shape from two (the
mode numbers). They could share a page if a glyph system is wanted.

### The Unframeable Now's report
![](unframeable-now-debrief.png)
![](unframeable-now-decay-report.png)
The arrow runs the other way here: the pulse sets the tempo, the loop is
a display of the day, the report draws the day. The sound that
accompanied a session leaves no trace in its report. It stays in the
candidate table at B at most, as the pipeline that records a browser
piece with its sound.

### Listed, no frame
- **Milo's sound rule** (`milo-and-me/`, Part VIII): music may move with
  trust and may not improve with it. A rule about sound's role in a
  game, not an image of sound.
- **Six Minutes Fast**: a companion video to a song, not in this
  repository. Precedent for the recorded form only.
