# I tried to tidy my feed. It tidied me.

*An op-ed in the voice of Kate: the player the welcome card of
[The Unframeable Now](../../the-unframeable-now/) describes, forty-one,
a team of six, 9:40 on a Tuesday. Kate is invented. She was played by
Claude, working with Jeff, in three recorded rounds on 12 October 2026
(30 seconds, 45 seconds, one minute), each on the build the previous
round's complaints had changed. Every number below is from those
rounds. The recordings and her round-by-round notes are on Jeff's
machine, not in the repository.*

---

I opened my feed on Tuesday night to clear it. I had twenty minutes
before I'd be too tired to want them, and I'd meant them for the garden
plan, or the record I keep not playing, or a painter I follow who had
posted half a canvas and a question. First, I thought, I'll just clear
the feed.

You know how that goes. I know how that goes. What I didn't know is
what it looks like from the outside, and this week I found out, because
someone built a thing that draws it.

It's a three-minute game. There's a phone on the right that looks like
mine: the group chat with 47 new messages about the venue, the live blog
with 1,200 updates, Slack at 6:48 pm with a "quick question," the school
portal with a form due Friday, again. Underneath those, in green, the
things I'd rather be looking at. The dahlias that want lifting before
the first frost. The record that is 41 minutes long when I have 40. You
use the phone like your phone: scroll, tap, like, hide, mute the group
chat, pull to refresh. I did all of it in the first twenty seconds
without thinking, which was the point.

On the left is a line. Time runs across it. Every item in the feed is a
dot. And every time you do anything, a violet dot appears above the
line, and an arc swings down from it to the moment, two seconds later,
when the thing you did comes back *into the feed as an item.* Mute the
group chat and a card arrives that says *you, muting the group chat.*
Search for the garden and a card arrives that says *you, searching for
the garden.* Underneath the line is a band showing how much of the feed
is you.

The first time, I thought the violet cards were a bug.

The second time I understood, and I tried to be careful, and the band
still climbed to 47%. Then I stopped moving, and I watched it fall. I
thought: good, it's clearing. It wasn't. The card at the end told me
what had actually happened, with my numbers: after my last move the band
fell from 47% to 25%, *not because anything of mine was removed, but
because the feed kept coming and buried me.* Stopping doesn't take you
out. It changes the proportions.

The third time I did it on purpose. Twenty seconds of what I'd normally
do, then hands off for forty. The band went to 36% and then slid to
nothing, and my purple cards drifted down the phone and off the bottom
under a poll and a sensor reading and a form. By the end there was no
sign I'd been there. All six of my moves had come back, every one, two
seconds later, no exceptions. And then every one of them had aged out,
like everything does.

Here are the three sentences the game offered me in my own voice, which
I ticked because I would actually say them:

*Every move I made to clear it became something to clear.*

*Muting the group chat didn't make room for the garden. It made room
for family.* (Seven times, in 25 seconds. The game counted.)

*Doing nothing isn't tidying either; it's being buried politely.*

And one it offered that I didn't tick, because it isn't true: that
liking the record was "the clearest thing I put in the feed all night."
It wasn't. The mute was. The game let me leave that one unticked, which
is the moment I decided it was honest.

Two things I want to say to the people who build the real ones.

First: the thing you call *engagement* is me trying to leave. Every
move I made was an attempt to get the feed out of the way of my evening,
and every one of them was recorded as interest and served back to me.
The only round in which the feed had none of me in it was the one in
which I did nothing at all, and in that round I also didn't get to the
garden plan. Not touching it isn't the same as having the twenty
minutes.

Second: the garden was there the whole time. In my longest round the
feed showed me 20 things, 8 of them things I'd actually have chosen, and
half of those sat below the cut-off the ranking had set for them. Nothing
I pressed lifted them. I liked the record and the record came twice as
often, which worked, and which is the only move I made that went my way,
and that move also came back as a card with my name on it and then that
card aged out too.

I don't have a fix. I have a sentence I didn't have on Tuesday, with
numbers I watched happen: the feed isn't a place I go to. It's something
I'm in. There was never an outside to tidy from.

Try it. Three minutes. Press the thing you'd press anyway, and watch the
arc.

## Postscript, three rounds later, with sound

They added sound, and I played three more: a normal day, a bad day, a
quiet evening. I'll keep this short because the recordings say it
better.

The bad day has a pulse under the music. I did my seven things in forty
seconds and then put my hands in my lap for fifty, and the pulse stayed
at 174 beats a minute the whole time, because the feed hadn't stopped.
On a bad day the tempo isn't your hands. It's the feed. Somewhere
around the fiftieth ping I stopped hearing them, and the game noticed
before I did.

The quiet evening is sixty beats a minute, and the mute actually buys
you twelve seconds of quiet before the little pop arrives to say you
did it, and the pop is funny instead of accusing, and when I muted the
group chat the garden came through, six times, which has never once
happened on my phone because I have never once had a quiet evening on
it.

With sound it stopped being a diagram and started being a Tuesday. Being
buried politely has a sound. It's a feed at 174 while you sit still.
