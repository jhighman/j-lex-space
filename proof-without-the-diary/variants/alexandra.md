# Proof Without the Diary

### Safeguards and Invariants, part three

---

I ended the last one with a promise I was not sure I could keep.

Show that something did not cross a boundary. Do it without building a
machine that watches the boundary.

Evidence about the lock. Not a warehouse of her secrets.

I have been turning that over for months, because stated plainly it sounds
like a trick question.

*How do you keep proof that a boundary held, without keeping the thing the
boundary was protecting?*

If you have arrived here first: this is a series about the difference between
what a company arranged and what stays true afterwards. Part one sat in a
child's bedroom, where a girl tells an AI companion her dog's name and
somebody later switches memory off, and asked nine questions about what that
switch actually establishes. The short answer was that a toggle is not a
fact. Part two asked how you would ever prove otherwise without building the
surveillance we were objecting to.

This one is what happened when we stopped writing and built something.

---

## The doorman who kept no list

We made a small machine. Not a companion, not anything a child would touch. A
few hundred lines whose only job was to answer one question: what must a
system write down when something crosses a boundary?

Think of it as a door with somebody standing at it.

A request arrives. The doorman checks it against the rules. If it passes, the
crossing is entered in a book. Only once there is an entry in the book —
only once somebody has the receipt for it — is the thing let through.

The one who asks cannot write in the book. Cannot open the door. Can ask.
That is all.

We built it that way round on purpose. Let something through and write it
down afterwards, and a power cut in between leaves you with something loose
in the house and nothing to say how it got in. Write first and let through
second, and the worst you are left with is an entry for something that never
came in.

An entry with no visitor is a discrepancy. A visitor with no entry is a hole.

Then we tested it, and it told us something we had not gone looking for.

The first version turned away bad requests and wrote nothing down. This felt
obviously right. The book is the trusted thing. Why would you fill it with
people who were not let in?

So we sent two visitors.

The first was turned away ninety-nine times before its hundredth request
finally qualified. The second arrived once and was admitted.

Then we read the book.

One entry. That was all it said. The same single line for both.

Ninety-nine refusals had not been hidden. They had not been redacted, or
summarised, or aged out. There was never anything to hide. They had simply
never existed as anything a later reader could find.

We had built a safeguard — turn away what does not qualify — and in the same
movement destroyed all evidence that the safeguard had ever been used.

---

The corrected version writes the refusals down. Same rules. Same door. Same
ninety-nine requests turned away on exactly the same grounds.

And now the book can tell you: *one visitor, a hundred attempts,
ninety-nine refusals. One visitor, one attempt, none.* Both eventually let
in. No longer the same story.

Nothing was made stricter. Nothing was loosened.

One more line in the book.

That is the difference between a door that can show you it was doing its job
and a door indistinguishable from an open one.

> A refusal nobody can see is indistinguishable from no refusal at all.

And there, unexpectedly, is the promise paid.

You do not need to keep the ninety-nine requests in order to show that
ninety-nine requests were refused. You need the fact of the knock, the fact
of the answer, and the fact that nothing came through.

A knock is a line in the book. What was said through the door is not.

---

## What we would not let it say

There is a second half, and without it this becomes the thing part two
refused.

A book that can record anything will eventually be asked to.

So we fixed the words. Not by policy — by construction. The record can write
twelve kinds of thing and a thirteenth cannot be written at all. There is no
word for a score. None for a mood. None for how she seemed today.

*How was she today* is not a question this system is able to answer. Not
declined. Not blank. Not answerable.

That is what makes the book safe to hand to a grown-up. Not a promise about
who reads it. What it is unable to say.

---

We tested this at full size on the least forgiving case we could think of: a
piece of software that watches a small child and reports to her parent. It is
a game, and there are no words in it, and a child walks through it with a
dog.

The dog has a name.

The record does not know he exists.

Not withheld. Not left blank. The book is never told, so there is nothing in
it to leak and nothing in it to refuse.

And because it genuinely does not know, something happens that we did not
have to enforce. At the end of the game there is an animal who will not come
out for anybody alone. She comes out when three friends speak for the child.

The dog cannot be one of them.

Not because we wrote a rule excluding him. Because a book that was never told
his name has no way to count him as a voice.

I have been trying since part one to say what an invariant is, and I think
that is the smallest honest example I have.

Not a promise that the name is safe.

Nowhere to put it.

---

## What we got wrong

I would rather tell you this part than have you find it.

The twelve words hold. They are enforced, and a test fails if they move.

Everything we wrote *about* them drifted.

The screen a parent reads told her the game could write down ten kinds of
thing, and that there was no eleventh — while it was writing twelve, two of
which were sitting in the list directly above the sentence. Underneath, the
function that fed that screen kept its own copy of ten. And a draft of a
report split one of the words into three that do not exist, and lost two that
do.

Three hand-written copies of one list. All describing something that was
perfectly true. All wrong within a day of each other.

The property never moved. Every description of it did.

And the descriptions were the only part she could read.

We had been guarding the machinery and never once guarding the sentences
about the machinery. They are generated now, and a test fails when they stop
matching.

There was a second one, smaller and worse.

That same screen was going to say *nothing is kept on this device*. Instead
of printing it, we made it go and look — walk everywhere the software is
allowed to write, and say what it finds.

Eighty-five thousand, nine hundred and eighty-three bytes. Nine files.
Pictures the graphics system had compiled. Nothing of hers in any of them.

And not nothing.

The sentence had been false since the first time the game drew anything, and
nothing would ever have contradicted it.

Then the screen listed all nine file names. They were meaningless strings,
and they pushed her child's actual afternoon off the bottom of the display.

Everything disclosed. Nothing conveyed.

## The same shape, larger

I had assumed all of this stayed in the bedroom.

Thirteen European governments have asked Brussels to clean up the rulebook:
consolidate, deduplicate, stop stacking new obligations on unresolved old
ones. That is a political request, not a change in law, and the difference
matters. The complaint underneath it is fair. Rules accumulate until
compliance becomes interpretation.

It sounds unrelated to a girl and a memory switch.

It is the same question, three sizes up.

*What can you take away without taking away the evidence that the boundary
was there?*

Did Monday cross into Thursday, and what would show it.

Did the refused request leave anything a later reader can find.

When obligations are consolidated, can the person affected still establish
what was done to her, and argue with it.

And here is the part the small machine settled for me. What fixed our door
was not a rule added or a rule taken away. Same authority. Same admissions.
Same refusals on the same grounds.

One line in the book.

Which means the argument being had — more rules or fewer, one instrument or
another — is nearly beside the point. A rulebook can get shorter and
stronger. It can get shorter and become impossible to demonstrate. Counting
what disappeared cannot tell those apart.

---

## Who ends up carrying it

When nothing was written down, the question does not go away. Somebody still
has to establish what happened.

It is never the one holding the records.

The parent has to establish that Monday survived.

The applicant has to establish that the gate got it wrong.

The citizen has to establish that her data was used for something else.

The work did not stop existing when the record did. It moved.

> **Burden displacement.** A system looks simpler because the complexity has
> left the operator's side of the boundary. It has not gone. It has been
> handed to the person trying to understand what happened to them.

The company has the records. The institution has the definitions. The
operator has the interfaces.

She has a question.

What makes this so hard to catch is that it cannot be seen from where the
decisions are made. Fewer forms, fewer hours, a cleaner diagram — every
number true, and not one of them describing what she can still find out. The
reform passes every test it set itself while the thing it was meant to
protect has quietly changed hands.

---

## What must remain true

> A governed crossing should leave enough lasting evidence for someone else
> to determine what was done, what became true as a result, under whose
> authority, against which rule, and what can be done about it — without
> keeping any more of her than that proof requires.

The first half answers the ninety-nine refusals nobody could see.

The last clause answers *keep everything*, which proves the lock by filming
everyone who comes near the door.

Two things I have had to be told, and will pass on.

*Independent* is a stronger word than *several*. We once claimed five
independent confirmations of a result. They shared a person, a record and a
process. One voice, five times. Distinctness is not independence — and a
simplification programme can produce four confirmations out of one pipeline
and call it agreement.

And evidence has to be able to say what it actually looked at. For a while
our own tests were not rebuilding the thing they were testing. They were
certifying a file. Nothing in the result would have told you.

---

## Her side of the week

We began with a girl, a switch, and a question that sounded simple.

Did Thursday remember Monday.

The small machine gave me a second one. If the boundary held — or if it
failed — what would be left that somebody else could look at?

And Europe is now asking a third, whether or not it has put it this way. As
the rules are simplified, what evidence of the boundary survives?

The answer cannot be to keep everything she ever said, forever, so that one
day somebody can prove we did not read it. That is the warehouse. That is
what we have been refusing since the beginning.

And it cannot be a tidier settings page, or a shorter rulebook, or our word
that the door stayed shut.

A safeguard says the door should stay closed.

An invariant says what must remain true.

Architecture leaves enough behind to show it.

She told it her dog's name.

The proof that it was not kept should not require keeping it.

Monday can stay on Monday's side of the week.

And Thursday does not need the diary to say so.

---

*The door, the tests and the wordless game are public, along with our own
record of what we got wrong — which is the more useful half. Nobody outside
the two of us has tried to break any of it, and no child has played the game.
Until both of those are true, this is a method that survived its authors,
which is the weakest thing you can say about one.*
