# Loop Gain — translation thesis

*Draft, 2026-10-28, for Jeff to rewrite in his words and Lex to review
against the four questions. Word count is kept under 500 for the body.
Drafted by Claude, working with Jeff.*

**1. The source.** Chladni, 1787: bow a plate strewn with sand and the
grains leave where the plate moves and gather where it is still, and each
tone draws its own figure. The finding we took is the structure under the
sand, not the sand: a continuous drive meets a threshold, and the pattern
reorganises rather than slides. Between resonances nothing happens; at one,
a new figure.

**2. What we carried across, and in which layer.** The structure, at the
finding layer, three times over on one signal. A feed's pings are read by
three grammars of return. In the first a gesture comes back once, as
itself, and does not act: the loop's reproduction number is zero and the
picture slides. In the second the same pings pass through a delay with
gain, so a return is excluded again and returns louder; the gain is the
reproduction number, and when it crosses one the echoes stop dying and
bloom into a howl. In the third the howl's settled frequency drives the
plate, and the sand gathers into its figure. Slope, threshold, form.

**3. What changed.** We kept the threshold and the sand, and the plate's
mathematics as the page already states it. We simplified the feed to a
headless run of its own model, replayed with its sound. We distorted the room: the loop's resonance is a
filter at 870 Hz placed by hand so that the howl would settle where the
plate has a figure, and a soft clip stands where a speaker's ceiling would
be. At Lex's filter we added Bridson's growth as the exterior and Atlas as
the piece's map. All of it is written on screen. We left out the body, Lex's allostatic
load, which the bench carries in four frames and the piece does not,
because a three-minute piece cannot hold a fifth variable and say what it
did. And the movements are cut by the clock, not by the sound; a truer
version would wait for the howl.

**4. What making it revealed.** That the two briefs we had been treating
as neighbours are one loop at two gains. We put the same question to both
systems during the week: as the drive is turned up, does the end state
slide or drop? The feed slid, and the knee in its curve turned out to be a
recovery threshold meeting an arrival rate, a continuous function. The
exclusion loop dropped, at exactly the propensity the reproduction number
predicted. Then a fourth frame, from Wegner's ironic process theory, showed
us what the reproduction number does not say: doubling the return rate
barely moves whether the loop runs away and halves the time a generation
takes. R0 says whether; the return rate says how fast. We proved the first
half as theorems and could only watch the second. That division, between
what a ratio can tell you and what needs a clock, is what the piece is
about, and we did not know it when we started.

---

**Source citation.** E. F. F. Chladni, *Entdeckungen über die Theorie des
Klanges* (Leipzig, 1787). The plate model follows Hans Jenny, *Kymatik*
(1967), for the round plate's Bessel modes and crossed standing waves for
the square. The loop's threshold is the branching-process criterion,
reproduction number one; its sound is the Larsen effect.
