# Rulings

Dated 26 September, from the co-author, on the reading above. Recorded here
because a decision that lives only in a mail thread is a decision nobody can
check afterwards. **None of it is a sign-off.**

## The rooms

When this document was written there were two rooms: the engine and the
bench. The public repository is a later child and is not read back into the
Saturday text. The rooms as they now stand: the bench stays the bench; the
new public repository is a second principal in code; and the engine's name
is the name of the work, not a repository handle to be confused with it —
the uses of it in section 4 are the blueprint's own, describing the body
inside the garment.

The substrate stays where it is. The whole engine is not woken on the back
of a translation. The FFI and IPC question section 7 raises is left in the
air on purpose, pending the two-key rule below: one change sometimes removes
another, and if it does not, it comes back.

## The Sentinel is enrolled, in two layers

Decided. A role is a vest, and a vest without a fingerprint can be worn by
whoever happens to be running the process. So the Sentinel stops being a
role and becomes an enrolled identity.

**Genesis, outside the ledger.** A signed Founding Roster beside the
charter. That document is the only place a vest is issued, and changing it
is an authors' act rather than a runtime write.

**Runtime assertion.** A process may speak as Sentinel only where its
fingerprint matches a row in that roster — and the engine checks a hash of
the roster, not that a file exists.

**Two keys, two jobs.** One is enrolled and visible, and is how the Sentinel
proves itself while running. The other is offline in a vault, never on the
machine and never in git; it amends the roster and the engine never sees it.
Neither author can rewrite the vest alone.

The part worth keeping is what the decision does *not* do. The question is
not closed by having been decided. It closes when the document exists. Until
then the hole named in section 7 is still a hole, and this record says so
rather than reporting a decision as though it were a fix.

## The turnstile does not print the price list

The Sentinel checks that a ticket is valid and that a price was paid. It
does not set the price.

`weight.py` and `outlives.py` showed the gap precisely: both can compute
once a reach or a term is given, and neither can say who is allowed to give
it. That is not something they can close from inside, and it is not a new
guardian. Authority for placing a price or a term belongs to a policy layer
outside the ledger, in the same family as the charter — meaning lives in the
charter and the assignment, authority lives with the authors, and placing
one is an act carrying a fingerprint rather than a number typed by whoever
is wearing the vest.

The Founding Roster is where that authority will sit, and it is not signed.
Until it is, **the calculator still accepts a number from the air.**

## Four checks are one vote

A correction to section 7, and the sharpest thing in the reply.

That section called five independent arrivals at the founding-roster
question the strongest structural claim either repository could make. It is
wrong. The checks are distinct; they are not independent. They share the
actor, the record and the process — a household, not four buildings. You can
look back and see how each member spoke, and on the register it stays one
unit with one vote. If two votes are ever needed that is a second principal,
not a second function in the same process.

This is the beam finding arriving against this document's own arithmetic.
Distinctness counted as independence, in a paper whose section 6 says in
plain words that counting rows instead of voices is the oldest hole here.
The pairing that established it stands, and the claim in section 7 does not.

## Promotion is not a reward for a green run

`experiments/` is the simulator. `framework/` is the road. A green run in
the simulator issues no licence, the four questions travel with the code if
it ever moves, and they are not retired by passing. The driver gets the
trainer after the exams — roster, policy layer, fingerprint — and not
because a test went quiet.

This milestone promotes nothing. Promotion would be a separate act, and it
is not on the table.

## What may be said about the engine

Stated the way a manufacturer states it, and not as a figure of speech: **do
not put a living thing in the drum.**

The harness attacked a translation. Four mutations turned it red, as they
should have. That is not a proof about the engine in the public repository,
and a green run against a Python target does not certify a Rust principal.
The mutation that failed to go red on its first run is on the record, and
being disclosed does not convert it into a blessing.

What is owed is unchanged and unpaid: a run against a build that is not the
target, by hands that did not write the harness. **Until that exists, nobody
gets to say the thing itself was tested.**
