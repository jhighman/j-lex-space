# Part X — How it holds together

A game with no words, no cutscenes and no dialogue still has to be one story
rather than nine errands. Nothing in it can *say* that the afternoon is
going somewhere, so cohesion has to be structural: the same fact showing up
in the picture, the mix and the record, and earlier acts having later
consequences the child can see.

Five things carry it. Three of them work. This part sets out all five and
then says which two did not, because the failures are the same failure and
it is the most useful thing in this document.

## 62. The climax is cumulative, and the currency is the walk

The warren descends toward the Moon Hare, who will not turn to face the
child on her own account. She needs the others to speak for her.

The mechanism is the design's whole argument compressed into one picture.
Her four places are the tide, and the tide answers to numbers like every
other creature's world. What lands on the water is not hers to give: **one
column of light for every friendship that was walked 1 → 2 → 3 → 4, in that
animal's own coat colour.** A friendship jumped to four leaves none, because
the walk did not happen. At three lights and full tide she turns round.

This is what converts nine isolated encounters into one quest. The colour on
the water is traceable to a specific animal in a specific burrow on a
specific afternoon, so the child is looking at her own history rendered as
light. And **Milo casts no light**, which states the rule without a word of
it: he was already hers, not a creature she had to earn.

## 63. History, not position

A story needs a route, not a location. Counting in order **walks** — posture,
light and mix ease over three quarters of a second. Pressing any other number
**jumps**: the place is genuinely true when she arrives, and the corridor did
not happen.

Neither is punished and there is no way to lose. But only a counted four
registers as a friendship, and only a friendship walked to sends an animal out
of the mouth with her. The distinction is invisible in any single frame and is
the entire difference between the game having a plot and having a state.

## 64. The acoustic descent

Each burrow's theme plays on the **approach** and stops at the threshold, so
the warren has a musical geography before the child is in any room.

Underneath the eight themes there was supposed to be a hidden unifying
centre: a G2 root at about 98 Hz in every one of them, an invisible tonal
floor holding the warren together. **It is not there, and §66 is the account
of how that was discovered.** What is there is a looser relation — four of
the eight in G, two in C minor whose dominant is G, two in F.

What does work, and works because it is a subtraction rather than an
assertion, is **the arrival at the bottom.** Entering the Moon Hare's chamber
strips the warm combustion, the lamp, and the music itself. In their place is
a cold, unlooped resonance, like air in a jar. Nothing announces that the
child has left the ordinary burrows; the ear simply reports that the rules
have changed, and hers is the only mouth in the warren with no tune on the
walk toward it.

## 65. The afternoon has a shape

Yard → walk → enter → stillness → rest → home.

The resolution is the homecoming. Every animal befriended that afternoon
gathers in the yard, stands together a moment, and leaves one by one — and
each turns back to look at the child before it goes. **The look back is the
whole beat.** An animal that simply walked off would read as leaving; turning
first is what makes it goodbye.

It resolves the afternoon without a score screen, and it is also where the
ephemerality axiom stops being a policy and becomes a feeling: they go
because friendships are not inventory, and the game's last image is of being
left by people who wanted to stay.

## 66. Milo, and the two claims that were not true

The fifth pillar is the one that was supposed to be simplest. **Milo is the
constant** — the friend she did not have to earn, present in every burrow,
noticing hurry before the wild animal does, a physical conscience in a game
with no voice to be one.

He had been refactored out of eight of the nine worlds.

When each creature became its own universe, the nine worlds were drawn and
the dog was carried into exactly one of them — the Moon Hare's chamber, and
only because the vouching rule needed him there to cast no light on her
water. In the other eight he was simply not drawn. **Nothing failed.** Nine
acceptance tests passed. The emotional constant of the game was present one
time in nine, in a build that had been played and screenshotted and written
about.

The second is the tonal centre. It had been measured on **two** files, found
true of both, written up as *the warren has a tonal centre*, and then written
into all eight generation prompts as *G2 drone, about 98 Hz*. Measured on all
eight delivered files: **one has its bass on G2.** The generator obliged the
character of every request and ignored the one number in it that could be
checked. Two of the eight — the rabbit and the fox — came back tonally
identical to each other, which nobody asked for and nobody noticed.

By the time both were checked, the G2 claim had already left the repository
and entered a written account of the game as an established fact. That is
precisely the distance a measured claim is entitled to travel and an
unmeasured one is not.

### What the two have in common

**A property asserted of a set, established on one or two of its members.**

Milo was specified as present everywhere and drawn once. G2 was measured
twice and prescribed eight times. And in the same two days, the rule that a
creature's mix must follow its own world — §58 — was named as a general
property and then fixed in the single creature it had been noticed in, so a
second creature carried the same defect for another day.

Three instances, one ingredient: **a sentence about *all* of something,
written after looking at *some* of it.**

No instrument in this project catches that. A test suite reports that a
system stopped matching its specification; every one of these matched.
A person playing catches it only by visiting the room where it is missing.
What would catch it is a check written as a question about a set — *which
worlds have no dog in them, which themes are not on G2* — and both of those
took under a minute once somebody thought to ask.

The two working pillars and the two broken ones came from the same design
mind on the same afternoons. The difference between them is not care. It is
that two of them had been counted.
