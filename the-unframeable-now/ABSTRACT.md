# The Unframeable Now: every frame applied re-enters as signal

*An abstract, drafted 2026-10-28 from the piece, the lab and the thread on
issue #6, for the brief's author to pull apart. Drafted with Claude,
working with Jeff. Nothing in it is a claim the record does not carry; the
numbers are the lab's, from `lab/README.md` and `lab/sweep.js`.*

Events reach a viewer already framed. A ranked feed supplies continuity,
valence, scale and implied resolution before the event arrives. This work
asks what the viewer's own gestures on such a feed do, and builds an
instrument to answer it. A simulated feed of seven channels, four that
ask something of her and three she would rather see, is ranked by stated
weights and cut-offs that favour the asks. Every gesture she makes, a
scroll, a like, a hide, a mute, a refresh, returns to the stream as an
item 2.2 seconds later. A band beneath the stream shows the share of the
feed that is her own echo. The only way to keep it flat is not to act.

A modelled state of four dimensions, attention, calm, agency and the
minutes she meant for something else, wears down under the session's
record, with every change traced to a named cause. Five modules add
physiology to the loop: a moment on a bad day that comes when her hands
are still, an allostatic floor that rises by a fixed step with each
moment and never recovers, asymmetric recovery on an exponential, a
fourteen-second refractory window, and one act of permanent removal, the
Empty Bin. A headless re-statement of the model runs personas by paces by
seeds, with every bad day drawn twice, with and without the moment,
matched by seed.

Across 240 sessions the moment's calm deficit persists as floor rather
than closing. The persona who tidies keeps attention and calm and loses
agency, because every hide and mute comes back. The persona who does
nothing keeps agency and still loses attention. What she empties is the
one thing that stays gone, and the act of emptying returns. Pushing the
feed's constants through a range, the interval between arrivals, the
asks' weight, the time a move takes to return, the time an item lasts,
finds no critical value: the end state holds flat above a knee near the
page's normal day and then slides, over about a decade of arrival rate,
for every persona; the one who never stops, already near the floor,
falls fastest. In this model the trap is a slope, not a threshold.

The constants are choices, not measurements. One process built the
piece, played its tester and wrote her notes. The model has one arrow,
pulse to tempo. The reverse arrow, a soundtrack that lags the pulse and
leads it down, is the experiment the mechanism supports and nobody has
run.
