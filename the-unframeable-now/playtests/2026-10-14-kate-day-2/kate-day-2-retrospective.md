## What changed, in one line each

- **The bin.** The one exit, four seconds wide, priced at two entries. Worth it on a normal day and a quiet one; on a bad day I missed the window and paid more than any choice would have cost.
- **The moment.** Yesterday it came at eight seconds for everyone. Today it waited for my hands if I let it (0:09) and stopped waiting if I didn't (0:16). You can't keep it off; you can only choose the sentence you're read afterwards.
- **The floor.** After the moment, calm's ceiling drops by 12 and stays dropped. It's the first thing in the game that doesn't wear off, and it's visible in the gold line without being told.
- **The aftershock.** Fourteen seconds of softer pings. The card says *not kindness*, which is the right thing to say.
- **The pulse remembers.** It takes twenty seconds to come down now instead of two. On a bad day that reads as truth. On a quiet evening it means my own hands can take calm to 37, which I believe and don't like.
- **The report stopped lying.** Agency only moves when something I did worked, and the bin counts. Round 8's complaint, answered.

## What I'd say to the builder, after twelve rounds

The game said on day 1 that there's no outside to tidy from. On day 2 it gave me one door, told me the price, and charged it. I don't think it gave anything away. The band still climbs with my hand; the group chat is gone and the news took its space; the thing I didn't ask for still arrives, and now it arrives when I've finally settled on something. That's closer to a Tuesday than anything on day 1.

Three asks:

1. The headline on the card should agree with the count beneath it when I've spent the bin on my own echo.
2. Put the bin's two prices in the welcome in numbers: what it costs to empty, and what it costs to let it close.
3. Say in the welcome that reading a card is being still. It's the natural thing to do, and it is exactly when it comes.

The sentence I'm keeping from day 2: **the one exit costs two entries, and it's still worth it.**
