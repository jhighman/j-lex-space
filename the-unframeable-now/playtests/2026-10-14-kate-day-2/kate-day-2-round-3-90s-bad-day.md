## What it told me

*At 0:16 something jumped at you. That was us, not the feed. You chose a bad day. It stopped waiting at sixteen seconds.* So it has two ways in: if I'm still, it comes when I'm still; if I'm never still, it comes at sixteen seconds regardless. Keeping my hands moving didn't keep it off. It chose which sentence I'd be read afterwards. That is my phone exactly: you can't out-busy it.

The fourteen seconds after are new. The pings went soft, the way a phone sounds from the next room, and the card has a line for it that I'd like to argue with and can't: *not kindness, aftershock.* It also says what it left: the floor under my calm rose by 12 points and did not come back down. In the replay you can see that. The gold line never gets back to where it was before 0:16, even in the long stretches where I did nothing but scroll.

Then the bin. I opened it at 0:40 meaning to take out the news, and on a bad day at 170 beats a minute, with cards arriving every second and a half and all of them edged in red, I could not pick one in four seconds. The window closed. The sound is a low square note held and a clamp of noise, and the pulse went to its ceiling, 173, and calm went from the forties to 10. *At 0:44 you opened the bin and let it close. You didn't choose; the clamp came down anyway: 30 points of calm, and the pulse to its ceiling.*

I want to be fair to it. Four seconds is enough on a normal day; I did it at 0:28 in round 1 without thinking. On a bad day four seconds is nothing, which is the point, and the price for not choosing is bigger than the price of any choice. That's true of real bad days too. But it's the first thing in the game that punished me for hesitating, and I'd like the welcome to say so before I press it, not after.

By the numbers: attention 100 to 18, calm 100 to 42 with a low of 10 at the clamp, agency 100 to 44. 91 things pinged and I stopped hearing them around 0:23. 19 of 19 moves came back. Muting the group chat made room for the news, 28 times.

## Against day 1

Round 5 on day 1 was the bad day where I stopped at 0:40 and the music didn't slow down. This time I never stopped, and it still didn't. The new thing is that the bad day now leaves a mark: a floor that stays up after the moment, and a clamp for a choice I didn't make in time. **On a bad day the feed doesn't wait for me, and the one thing it offers me, it offers for four seconds.**

**For the builder:** the aftershock reads. The clamp reads. Put the price of the bin closing in the welcome in plain numbers, and consider a hair more than four seconds on a bad day, or at least say that four seconds at this pace is the test.
