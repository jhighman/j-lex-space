## The bin

The pad goes red and starts counting: *Tap one card · 3.4*. The sound under it is two notes pulling apart and climbing, held, like a kettle that's been left. The cards get a dashed red edge, all of them, which is the game saying: any of these, but one. I knew which. I've known for two days.

I tapped it and the card went, and a chime came down in four high notes, and the pulse dropped. I felt the drop in the music before I read it in the numbers: 101 beats a minute at the refresh, back under 90 after. The caption said: *Gone: the group chat. It will not come back. Emptying it was a move, and that will.*

And it did. Two seconds later: *you, opening the bin*. Two seconds after that: *you, emptying the group chat for good*. Two violet cards, with my name on them, for the one thing I'd taken out. The band stepped up twice. That's the deal, and the card afterwards put it plainly: *Opening the bin and emptying it both came back as items, 2.2 seconds later, like everything else.*

I'm fine with the deal. I'd pay two moves for the group chat every night of my life.

## What it told me

*12 moves. All of them came back. By the end, 26% of your feed was you.* Peak 44% at 0:15, which is the refresh.

The report: attention 100 to 90, calm 100 to 85, agency 100 to 88. Those are the gentlest numbers I've had. The replay shows why: calm dipped to 59 around the refresh and then the bin lifted it, a visible step up at 0:28 in the gold line, the first time anything I did has moved a line *up* that wasn't the garden like. Agency the same: down in steps to 76 with each move, then a step to 88 when the bin emptied. The report says that's the bin, and names it: *you, emptying the group chat for good.*

Disorder 0.61 at the end, which is high for a normal day, and the field confirms it: a lot of grey. I think the bin did that too. Taking one card out and adding two of mine makes the field more mixed, not less. The definition is stated and I can't argue with it. But it's a strange thing to be told that cleaning up raised the disorder, and I'd like the card to say why, in words, the way it does for everything else.

## Against day 1

Day 1, round 2, same length, same day: I stopped at 22 seconds and watched the band fall and learned that stopping is being buried politely. This time I didn't stop; I spent the bin. The band ended at 26% instead of falling to nothing, because I kept putting myself in it. Calm ended higher than any round on day 1, and the only reason is the thing I took out.

So here is the sentence for day 2, round 1: **the one exit costs two entries, and it's still worth it.**

**For the builder:** the countdown sound is right, the chime is right, the red edge on every card is right. One thing: when the four seconds end on their own I haven't seen yet what happens, and I'd like the welcome to tell me more plainly that it's worse than not pressing. "It closes on you" is coy. Say what it costs.
