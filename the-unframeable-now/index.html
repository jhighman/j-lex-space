The Unframeable Now · a two-minute experiment

Try to tidy your feed without leaving a trace of yourself in it.

What this is
Your feed, invented but true to shape. The group chat, the news, work after hours, the school portal, all asking. Underneath them, what you'd rather be looking at: the garden, a painter's half-finished canvas, a record you keep meaning to play. Real feed machinery runs it: a ranking that favours whatever asks something of you, a cut-off, a lifetime, links.
What you do
Tidy it the way you always do: search for the garden, mute the threads, ask why you're seeing this, size things up, keep refreshing. The pads at the bottom are what you'd press anyway.
What to watch
Time runs left to right. Every move you make is a violet mark above the stream, with an arc to where it comes back into the stream two seconds later. The band at the bottom is how much of the stream is you.
What you'll find
You can't keep the band flat. There is no outside to tidy from: every tidy-up, even a search, goes back in. Every real feed does this with your clicks, silently. Here it's drawn.

The session runs three minutes on its own clock, which stops when you pause. It ends by itself, or when you press I'm done. Then the line tells you what it saw.

Nothing leaves your machine. The items are invented and name nobody. Press ? intro to come back here.
