The Unframeable Now · a three-minute experiment

Try to tidy your feed without leaving a trace of yourself in it.

Who you are
It's 9:40 on a Tuesday. You're forty-one, you run a team of six, and your phone is also where your mother, your daughter's school and the group chat find you. The kitchen is done. You have twenty minutes before you're too tired to want them, and you meant them for the garden plan, or the record, or the painter's question. You open the feed first. Just to clear it.
What this is
That feed, invented but true to shape. The group chat, the news, work after hours, the school portal, all asking; underneath them, the garden, the canvas, the record. Real feed machinery runs it: a ranking that favours whatever asks something of you, a cut-off, a lifetime, links.
What you do
Everything you can do is on the bar at the bottom of the screen. Left to right:
  • ▶Play and pause. The clock stops while you're paused.
  • the phoneUse it like yours: scroll; tap a card to open it, then ♡ like it, ✕ hide it, mute its channel, or ask why; pull down to refresh; search. The feed learns from every one of these, the way yours does.
  • Mute the threadsDrops the links and timestamps; ranking stops holding anything in place.
  • Why am I seeing this?Shows each item's cut-off (red) and how long it has left (blue).
  • Size things upSizes items by their number: one text from your daughter beside 1,200 updates.
  • Keep refreshingPulls everything back to now, again and again.
  • search… try gardenType a word. Matches stay bright; the rest dims but stays.
  • paceQuiet evening, normal day, or bad day: one thing every second and a half.
Press a pad again to turn it off. Every one of these is a move.
What to watch
Your phone, on the right, is the feed as you see it. The line, on the left, is what is happening to it: time runs left to right, and every move you make is a violet mark above the stream, with an arc to where it comes back into the feed two seconds later. The band at the bottom is how much of the feed is you.
What you'll find
You can't keep the band flat. There is no outside to tidy from: every tidy-up, even a search, goes back in. Every real feed does this with your clicks, silently. Here it's drawn.

Three minutes on the feed's own clock; it ends by itself or when you press I'm done, and then the line tells you what it saw. Nothing leaves your machine; the items are invented and name nobody. ? intro brings this back.
